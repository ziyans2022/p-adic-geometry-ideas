# arXiv Search Guide for P-adic Geometry

This guide gives targeted search strategies to find recent preprints.
The field moves fast — always check arXiv before committing to a problem.

-----

## Primary Categories to Watch

|Category   |Scope                                                                   |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|**math.NT**|Number theory — most p-adic Hodge theory, Langlands, prismatic          |
|**math.AG**|Algebraic geometry — perfectoid spaces, rigid geometry, prismatic stacks|
|**math.RT**|Representation theory — p-adic Langlands, completed cohomology          |

For most problems in this skill, search **math.NT + math.AG** simultaneously.

## Recommended Search Workflow

1. **Daily/weekly**: Subscribe to math.NT and math.AG new submissions on arXiv.org
1. **Before starting a project**: Search the above terms on [arXiv search](https://arxiv.org/search/) and filter to last 2 years
1. **Check citations**: Use [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org) or [MathSciNet](https://mathscinet.ams.org) to find papers citing key references
1. **Key authors to follow** (check their recent preprints directly):
- Bhargav Bhatt (U Michigan / IAS)
- Peter Scholze (MPIM Bonn)
- Laurent Fargues (IMJ-PRG)
- David Hansen (NUS / MPIM)
- Johannes Anschütz (U Münster)
- Arthur-César Le Bras (IRMA Strasbourg)
- Eugen Hellmann (U Münster)
- Xinwen Zhu (Stanford)
- Ana Caraiani (Imperial College)
- Matthew Emerton (U Chicago)
- Ben Heuer (Leibniz U. Hannover) — bheuer.github.io for preprints
- Daxin Xu (AMSS Beijing) — p-adic non-abelian Hodge theory
- Yu Min & Yupeng Wang — prismatic approach to Simpson
- Annette Werner (Goethe U. Frankfurt) — non-archimedean vector bundles

- **Séminaire Bourbaki**: exposés on major results, freely available
- **Persiflage blog** (Frank Calegari): commentary on current developments in number theory

## How to Assess Whether a Problem Is Still Open

1. Search arXiv for the exact keywords of the problem
1. Check MathSciNet reviews citing the original papers
1. Look at the most recent papers by the main authors — do they reference this as open?
1. Check MathOverflow: many folklore open questions are discussed there
1. When in doubt, email an expert — the community is generally responsive
